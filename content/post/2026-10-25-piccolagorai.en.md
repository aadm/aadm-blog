I bought a gravel bike, and I organized a tour with friends around a lesser group of mountains in Trentino (Italy), called the Lagorai group.

Now, if the mountains were gods I would be instantly struck by thunderbolts for having called it a "lesser" mountain. Despite not being as famous as the nearby Dolomites, these are serious mountains, less spectacular perhaps but wilder; there are no crowds on its trails, no fancy mountain huts serving prosecco with pumping music in the background. And if you happen to be in Bellamonte, you would certainly notice these dark and imposing lines of jagged peaks, often obscured by heavy clouds.

And there is a gravel tour called the [Gran Lagorai](https://www.granlagorai.it/), that has caught my attention for some time now. I don't know if people are aware of this tour, I don't know the people that found this route, connected the dots and prepared the excellent website. The following quotes are copied straight from their excellent manifesto:


> The Granlagorai Bike Adventure is a permanent bikepacking route [...] The Lagorai still preserves a wild soul: a rare, scarcely populated wilderness. We believe that those who experience the mountains can do so respectfully, without harming their biodiversity or identity [...] The route is built on two principles: freedom and respect.[...] Honor the mountains and their fragile harmony. Prepare well, set off whenever you wish, and experience the Lagorai with respect: your adventure is waiting.

And this is indeed a gran tour: it's 280 km with more than 8000 m of climbing. I have never done anything like this so I shrunk the tour, called it "piccolo" (small) and invited 3 friends to share the adventure.

(yes I know it may seem a stretch to call a 3 days bike ride with comfortable stops in nice refugios an "adventure" but the benchmark here is the 10 minute daily commute).

## "peak" lagorai?

Now what is with the name of this post. It just means _small_ ("piccolo") Lagorai tour. What I did was look up the original route, make a few changes, then call Paolo, Phil and Max with only one rule: all on gravel bikes. Because this was the first proper ride on my new Fairlight Secan.

(During the back and forth on our whatsapp group, Phil kept over-analysing the route and extract meaningless statistics like average slope, projected riding times etc, and he was half-jokingly suggesting that the best bike would have been a mountain bike not a gravel bike; in the end he only changed his chainring to a 36t, while the rest of us rode what we had, which is pretty conventional standard Shimano groupset with 40t rings paired to 12 speed cassettes).

## day 1

I honestly thought it was a good start for our ride (38km, 1850m of climbing). But this is what it really was:

* a relentless climb up to Passo Langhen
* a very long, very fast road descent (with Paolo terrorizing the fellow bikers with motogp-style overtakes)
* a ten minute uber technical trail, made it spicier by wobbling bags and tall seats
* another (smaller) climb to our final destination, the Hotel SAT Lagorai

## day 2

Day 2 is the longest day. Phil decides to get a taxi and will wait for us at Capanna Cervino, above (way above) San Martino di Castrozza. We will get there after riding 66km with ~2400m of climbing.

{{<figure src="media/piccolagorai_IMG_0755.jpg" caption="Max joins the fun party">}}

Also, notice the Tracks 6000 banner. This rifugio is the starting place for a crazy and secretive long-distance event that is annually around here. [Look it up](https://www.tracks6000.com/) if you dare.

I happened on a quote taken from a NYT article written in 1878 (!) while I was working on these photos:

_"The bicyclist appropriates the aesthetic meaning of the winged heel with which the ancients endowed the messenger of the gods. [...] The bicycle ranks among those gifts of science to man [...] Why should he go a-foot then he can ride a mustang of steel who knows his rider and never needs a lasso? This is no boy's plaything. There are plenty of men who prefer it on some accounts to a horse or yacht."_
